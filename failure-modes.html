Failure Mode Catalog

Five recurring transparency problems identified through comparative analysis of 40 online rental listing artifacts. These definitions correspond to the research dataset.

Purpose

In human systems engineering, failure modes describe conditions that can interfere with successful system use. This catalog identifies information-presentation problems that may affect renters' ability to evaluate affordability, eligibility, and housing options.

Each failure mode includes a definition, an illustrative example, and a potential decision risk. Examples explain the categories rather than document additional observations.

Research Failure Modes

FMC-101: Price Decoupling

Advertised rent is separated from required recurring charges, making the displayed price incomplete for monthly budgeting.

Illustrative example: Base rent appears before mandatory parking, trash, or technology charges.

Decision risk: A renter may underestimate recurring housing costs.

FMC-102: Qualification Gating

Income, credit, guarantor, or screening requirements are not readily visible before an application decision.

Illustrative example: Qualification criteria appear only after a separate inquiry or later step.

Decision risk: A renter may spend time or application fees on an unsuitable listing.

FMC-103: Concession Masking

A temporary promotion is emphasized without equally clear explanation of the ongoing, nonpromotional cost.

Illustrative example: A free-month offer is more prominent than the regular monthly obligation.

Decision risk: A renter may misjudge longer-term affordability.

FMC-104: Information Fragmentation

Decision-relevant costs and conditions are distributed across multiple sections, pages, or documents.

Illustrative example: Rent, deposits, utilities, and eligibility information appear in different locations.

Decision risk: A renter must search for, remember, and reconcile information from several sources.

FMC-105: Utility Ambiguity

Responsibility for utilities or expected utility costs is absent, vague, or not estimated.

Illustrative example: A listing states that utilities vary without providing a usable estimate or range.

Decision risk: A renter cannot confidently estimate total recurring housing costs.

Findings From the Artifact Dataset

The exploratory dataset included 40 rental listing records from Apartments.com, Zillow, Redfin, and Apartment List. The following counts describe only those collected records.

  • FMC-101 — Price Decoupling: 28 of 40 (70%)
  • FMC-102 — Qualification Gating: 34 of 40 (85%)
  • FMC-103 — Concession Masking: 28 of 40 (70%)
  • FMC-104 — Information Fragmentation: 40 of 40 (100%)
  • FMC-105 — Utility Ambiguity: 38 of 40 (95%)

Multiple failure modes could occur within the same listing. These descriptive findings should not be interpreted as estimates of their prevalence across rental platforms or the broader housing market.

Connection to the Housing Transparency Standard

The failure modes identify problems that the proposed Housing Transparency Standard seeks to address. Price Decoupling and Utility Ambiguity concern financial cost clarity. Qualification Gating concerns eligibility disclosure. Concession Masking concerns pricing over time, while Information Fragmentation concerns the accessibility and organization of decision-relevant information.

Design Implications

The findings informed the proposed True-Cost Estimator and related design recommendations. These concepts emphasize consolidated recurring costs, upfront move-in expenses, and earlier communication of qualification requirements.

The prototype illustrates a possible design response. Its effectiveness in reducing renter decision errors has not been established through controlled testing.
